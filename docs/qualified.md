## Hard, factual disqualifiers — safe for the AI to auto-decline

These require no interpretation; the front desk applied them identically every time they came up:

1. **Outside service area** (Pune/PCMC) — T03
2. **Below minimum commercial size** (~500 sq ft) — T18
3. **Design-only / advisory request, no execution** — T04
4. **Out-of-scope category**: hospitality, gym/fitness — T19, W07
5. **Timeline impossible given minimum design+execution lead time** — T07

## Soft, judgment calls — must flag to human review, never auto-decline

1. **Budget appears mismatched with scope** (T10: ₹1–1.5L for a full kitchen + bedroom execution was declined by the front desk, but this requires judging *how far* below range is a real mismatch vs. a prospect who hasn't yet understood scope — a human should set that line, not the AI guessing per-call)
2. **Ambiguous decision-maker situation** (e.g. T14: adult child enquiring on behalf of parents who don't use phones — front desk proceeded but this is a judgment call about whether to treat the caller as qualified)
3. **Unclear or partial project scope** where the caller hasn't articulated enough to apply the above rules confidently

## What counts as a strong qualified lead (positive signal, safe to auto-approve)

- Clear service-area match, in-scope category, feasible timeline, and either a stated budget consistent with `pricing.md` ranges or no budget objection raised
- Caller is the decision-maker (or all decision-makers will attend consultation — see T14's resolution)
